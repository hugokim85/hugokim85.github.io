Yongho (Hugo) Kim

Principal Engineer | Materials & Process Development

Samsung Electronics · Global Technology Research Center · Suwon, South Korea

Developing new materials and manufacturing processes, from concept to mass production.

I am a Ph.D. engineer with 11+ years of experience turning materials research into manufacturable products. At Samsung Electronics’ Global Technology Research Center, I work across material formulation, component design, process development, and production validation. My focus is connecting what a material can do in the laboratory with what a product needs in everyday use and high-volume manufacturing.

My current work addresses two closely connected challenges in foldable devices: reducing weight and managing heat within limited space. I lead CFRP-based lightweighting programs through the Galaxy Z Fold8 and am developing a thermal-management component intended for the Galaxy Z Fold9. I have also led FBR-ALD-based quantum-dot durability development for new QLED TVs, alongside material, process, and structural development for new QLED form factors.

Earlier in my career, I developed sustainable polymers for automotive interiors and helped bring waterproof coatings and material-recovery processes into electronics production. Those experiences shaped how I approach new technology: define the product requirement, understand material and process behavior, and work with design teams, suppliers, and manufacturing sites to make the solution practical. My production deployments span Brazil, Vietnam, and India.

Mar 2023 – Present

Principal Engineer

Samsung Electronics · Global Technology Research Center

Suwon, South Korea

  • Foldable-device lightweighting. Lead CFRP material and component development for foldable smartphones, with programs extending through the Galaxy Z Fold8. My work includes display backplates and hinge components, linking composite selection, Siemens NX component and laminate layup design, and molding-process development. I sourced prepregs at the required thickness from global suppliers and resolved plate warpage through on-site molding-process optimization. Previous developments achieved a 10 g weight reduction and reduced component thickness.
  • Thermal management for foldable devices. Earlier development of thermal-management polymer parts and heat-dissipation resin achieved a 0.7°C reduction in application processor temperature. I am currently developing a separate heat-dissipation component intended for the Galaxy Z Fold9, focusing on thermally conductive materials that spread heat from the application processor. This ongoing development connects material performance with component geometry and the space constraints of a foldable device.
  • Quantum-dot durability and new QLED form factors. Led development using fluidized-bed reactor atomic layer deposition (FBR-ALD) to improve quantum-dot durability for new QLED TVs. Also led materials and structural-design selection, process development, and Product Verification (PV) for new QLED form factors. To assess a proposed form factor’s feasibility, I worked with colleagues on optical-design and simulation reviews and ran validation experiments, building the evidence needed to advance the concept into development.
  • From concept to production readiness. Lead new product introduction (NPI) through material validation, process trials, supplier qualification, and EVT/DVT/PVT. Work with product design and manufacturing teams to translate structural, functional, and appearance requirements into practical material and process choices.
  • Experiment-led development. Apply Design of Experiments (DOE), including response surface methodology (RSM) and central composite designs (CCD), to investigate material and process variables. Use Minitab, Python, and AI-assisted material-property simulation and experiment planning to guide trials and reduce unnecessary experimental runs.
  • Supplier collaboration. Co-develop and qualify materials and processes with global suppliers, working through technical evaluation, process capability, and production-readiness requirements so that promising concepts can progress toward high-volume manufacturing.

Jul 2017 – Feb 2023

Senior Engineer

Samsung Electronics · Global Technology Research Center

Suwon, South Korea

  • Protective coatings for electronics. Developed silicone conformal-coating materials and processes to address moisture-driven corrosion on PCB assemblies and connectors. I combined resin development with selective dispensing and coating-thickness control. To resolve concerns about connector function, I built a test setup to evaluate connector mating and signal integrity and used comparative results to refine and validate the coating approach. Material evaluation, process optimization, and standard operating procedures (SOPs) supported adoption across mobile phones and wireless-earbud charging cradles.
  • Global production deployment. Set up specialized coating processes and production lines in Brazil, Vietnam, and India, considering takt time, production volume, and equipment investment. During COVID-19 logistics constraints, I established a manual pilot line to validate dispensing parameters before automated equipment arrived, enabling production ramp-up on schedule. The coating deployments delivered $35M in warranty savings in Brazil within two years.
  • Simplifying sealing and assembly. Proposed dispensing and curing urethane-acrylate material around a USB port in place of a manually installed rubber gasket. This enabled dispenser-based automation and reduced assembly labor. I subsequently extended the approach to replace waterproof tapes with bonding processes in other applications.
  • Superhydrophobic coatings for manufacturing tooling. Applied superhydrophobic coatings to bonding-nozzle surfaces using iCVD, reducing annual manufacturing costs by $0.5M.
  • Material recovery at manufacturing scale. Developed regeneration and recovery technologies for production consumables and defective components, then scaled the processes across manufacturing sites in Vietnam. Optimizing recovery processes and improving yield reduced annual manufacturing costs by $2.2M.

Feb 2015 – Jun 2017

Senior Engineer

Hyundai Motor Group · Material Research Center

Hwaseong, South Korea

  • Automotive polymer development. Developed and mass-produced injection-molded PP, PA, and TPE composites for automotive interiors, balancing mechanical performance, surface appearance, durability, and manufacturability.
  • Surface quality and molding compatibility. Engineered a scratch- and soiling-resistant PP/talc compound for existing shared molds. When the formulation introduced filling and shrinkage issues, I ran DOE, collected viscosity data, and worked with suppliers and simulation partners to review Moldflow results. Production trials guided formulation and PP-grade selection, and a melt flow index (MFI) specification helped control manufacturability. The production launch contributed to improved U.S. Initial Quality Study (IQS) results from 92 to 74 problems per 100 vehicles (PP100).
  • Lightweight door trims without sacrificing stiffness. Combined fine-talc reinforcement with gas-counterpressure foam injection molding to balance stiffness, weight, and the surface quality required for unpainted interiors. Material and molding optimization delivered approximately 15% lower part weight while maintaining stiffness, contributing to the 2015 NET Award.
  • Field-failure investigation. Investigated returned air-vent bezels with cracking issues using mechanical testing, heat-deflection temperature (HDT), FTIR, and SEM examination of cross-sections. The analysis identified nonconforming material content and supported supplier corrective action.
  • Bio-based materials for vehicle interiors. Formulated and industrialized a composite containing approximately 40% seaweed-derived polymer, using inorganic fillers to improve mechanical performance for hybrid and fuel-cell vehicle (FCEV) interior applications.
